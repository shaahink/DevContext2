## 5. What these numbers do and do not license

- The bootstrap resamples **6 questions**. A percentile CI from n=6 is wide and lumpy by
  construction; it is reported because the design pre-registered it, not because 6 questions
  settle anything.
- The non-inferiority bar in DESIGN 4.2 is `-0.05`. With 18 items per arm, the *widest*
  the pilot can be right is a Wilson lower bound of 86.9% at a perfect 18/18, so the
  90% lower bound on a paired difference cannot reach `-0.05` at this n **whatever the answers**.
  A pilot cannot pass this bar; that is a statement about the sample size, not about the tools.
- Rank methods and medians throughout. No t-test, no mean over raw dollars.
- The one censored run (`eshop-c1/M/rep1`, budget exhausted) is kept and counted everywhere.

## 6. Mapping onto the DESIGN 5 decision rule

| Branch | Pre-registered condition | Computed | Met |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accelerator** | non-inferior AND cost CI upper < -0.32 | upper = 0.346 | no |
| **Primer** | 90% CI lower on d-accuracy > +0.05, cost not reduced | lower = -0.131 | no |
| **Null** | neither CI excludes zero | cost 95% [-0.423, 0.346], acc 90% [-0.131, 0.131] | YES |
| **Regression** | non-inferiority fails | lower = -0.131 vs bar -0.05 | fires - read the caveat |

> **The Regression branch fires on a power artifact and must not be reported as a regression.**
> Arms B and G are *exactly tied*: 18/18 concordant-correct, **zero discordant pairs**
> (b=0, c=0), exact McNemar p=1.0000. Nothing in the data says arm B is less accurate than
> arm G on a single item. The branch fires because the tightest interval 18 pairs can produce is
> +/-0.131, and the pre-registered bar is +/-0.05. Calling that a regression would be
> reporting the sample size as if it were a finding.
>
> The honest pilot-scale reading is **Null**: the cost CI contains zero, the accuracy
> contrast IS zero, and the design's own bars need the 360-run full study to be reachable.

Arm M is not part of the primary contrast, but it is the sharpest signal in the pilot and
the write-up should not bury it: 
arm M scores **12/18** (66.7%) against 18/18 for arm G, at **1.508x** the cost
(Wilcoxon p=0.0313, the smallest value n=6 can produce). Its failures are total on 2 question(s):
`eshop-c1` (class C) 0/3, `eshop-f1` (class F) 0/3. Class F is the design's own
grep-favouring control, and DESIGN 5's note that "if the MCP arm wins here, the harness is wrong"
cuts both ways: it loses here, which is evidence the harness is measuring what it claims to.
